# Unit IV – Quick Revision
## Cryptocurrency: Bitcoin and Token | 410243

### 1. Cryptocurrency
Digital/virtual asset secured using cryptography and commonly operated on blockchain or distributed ledger technology.

**Common categories and examples**
- Payment: BTC, LTC
- Platform: ETH, SOL
- Utility: BAT
- Security: tokenized real-estate investment
- Stablecoin: USDT, USDC
- Privacy: XMR
- Governance: UNI
- Meme: DOGE, SHIB

Categories may overlap.

### 2. Coin vs Token
- Coin: native to its own blockchain (BTC, ETH).
- Token: created on an existing blockchain (USDT, UNI, BAT).

### 3. MetaMask
- Developed by Consensys.
- Non-custodial wallet; browser extension and mobile app.
- Manages keys/assets and connects to supported DApps.
- Typical flow: Install → Create wallet → Back up recovery phrase → Connect DApp → Review transaction → Sign → Broadcast.
- Risks: phishing, malicious approvals, lost recovery phrase, variable gas fees.

### 4. Cryptocurrency Wallet
A wallet manages cryptographic keys; coins are recorded on the blockchain.
- Public address: receive assets.
- Private key: authorize transactions.

**Hot wallet:** keys managed in an internet-connected environment. Examples: MetaMask, Trust Wallet, web/desktop/mobile wallets.

**Cold wallet:** private keys kept offline. Examples: Ledger, Trezor, paper wallet.

### 5. Custodial vs Non-Custodial
- Custodial: third party controls private keys; provider may support account recovery. Example: exchange account wallet.
- Non-custodial: user controls keys and recovery phrase. Examples: MetaMask, Trust Wallet, hardware wallets.
- Core difference: who controls the private keys.

### 6. Coinbase vs Binance
- Both are centralized cryptocurrency exchanges.
- Coinbase: known for simple beginner-friendly exchange experience.
- Binance: broad trading products and interfaces; BNB is associated with its ecosystem.
- Coinbase Wallet and Binance Web3 Wallet are separate self-custody products, not the same as exchange account wallets.
- Fees, products and availability vary by jurisdiction.

### 7. Bitcoin
- White paper: 2008; network launched: January 2009.
- Pseudonym: Satoshi Nakamoto.
- Maximum supply: 21 million BTC.
- 1 BTC = 100,000,000 satoshis.
- Uses peer-to-peer network and Proof of Work.
- Transaction flow: Create → Authorize → Broadcast → Verify → Mine → Validate block → Confirm.
- Advantages: decentralization, transparency, peer-to-peer transfers.
- Limitations: volatility, variable fees/confirmation time, mining energy use, key-loss risk.

### 8. MetaMask vs Coinbase Wallet
- Both are non-custodial/self-custody wallets.
- MetaMask: Consensys; widely used in Ethereum/EVM Web3.
- Coinbase Wallet: Coinbase; self-custody wallet product.
- Compare developer, platforms, supported networks, DApp connectivity, features and recovery options.
- Exact network support/features may change by version.

### 9. Cryptocurrency Uses
Payments, cross-border transfers, trading/investment, DeFi, smart contracts, NFTs and access to certain digital financial services.

### 10. Token Types
- Utility: access to services (BAT).
- Security: represents financial/investment rights.
- Governance: voting rights (UNI).
- Stablecoin: stable-value design (USDT, USDC).
- NFT: unique digital representation/collectible.

### Must-Remember Facts
- Wallets manage keys, not physical coins.
- Custodial = provider controls keys.
- Non-custodial = user controls keys.
- Hot = online environment; cold = offline keys.
- Bitcoin uses Proof of Work.
- Bitcoin maximum supply = 21 million.
- Coin = own blockchain; token = existing blockchain.
- Never share a private key or recovery phrase.
