## Q1. List and Explain the Different Types of Cryptocurrencies with Examples. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
Cryptocurrency is a digital or virtual asset that uses cryptographic techniques to secure transactions. It operates using blockchain or another distributed ledger technology. Cryptocurrencies can be classified according to their purpose and functionality.

### 2. Types of Cryptocurrencies

1. **Payment Cryptocurrencies:** Designed mainly to transfer value and make digital payments. Examples: Bitcoin (BTC), Litecoin (LTC).
2. **Platform Cryptocurrencies:** Used to operate blockchain platforms and pay for network transactions or computation. Examples: Ether (ETH) on Ethereum, SOL on Solana.
3. **Utility Tokens:** Provide access to a product, service or feature within a blockchain ecosystem. Example: Basic Attention Token (BAT) in the Brave ecosystem.
4. **Security Tokens:** Represent investment or financial rights in an underlying asset, subject to applicable securities laws. Example: A token representing fractional ownership in a tokenized real-estate investment.
5. **Stablecoins:** Designed to maintain a relatively stable value, often by being linked to a fiat currency. Examples: USDT (Tether), USDC.
6. **Privacy Coins:** Include features intended to improve transaction privacy. Example: Monero (XMR).
7. **Governance Tokens:** Allow holders to participate in certain project or protocol governance decisions. Example: UNI of Uniswap.
8. **Meme Coins:** Often originate from internet memes or online communities and may be driven substantially by community interest. Examples: Dogecoin (DOGE), Shiba Inu (SHIB).

### 3. Difference Between Coin and Token

| Parameter | Coin | Token |
|---|---|---|
| Blockchain | Operates on its own blockchain. | Created on an existing blockchain. |
| Purpose | Often used for payments and native network operations. | May represent utility, governance, ownership or other rights. |
| Creation | Requires a native blockchain network. | Commonly created using smart contracts or token standards. |
| Examples | BTC, ETH. | USDT, UNI, BAT. |

**Note:** These categories can overlap; the classification depends on a cryptocurrency's design and use. A coin and a token are not interchangeable terms.

### Conclusion
Cryptocurrencies serve different purposes, including payments, platform operations, utility, investment, privacy, governance and digital communities. Their functions and risks depend on their underlying design.

## Q2. What is MetaMask? Explain its Features, Working, Applications, Benefits and Drawbacks. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
MetaMask is a non-custodial cryptocurrency wallet developed by Consensys. It is available as a browser extension and mobile application. It helps users manage digital assets and connect to supported blockchain networks and decentralized applications (DApps). In a non-custodial wallet, users are responsible for protecting their recovery phrase and keys.

### 2. Features
1. **Wallet Management:** Creates and manages cryptocurrency accounts and wallet addresses.
2. **Send and Receive:** Allows users to send and receive supported cryptocurrencies and tokens.
3. **DApp Connectivity:** Connects users to decentralized applications.
4. **Network Support:** Allows users to access Ethereum and supported EVM-compatible networks, along with other supported networks and assets.
5. **Transaction Approval:** Displays transaction details for users to review and approve.
6. **Token Management:** Supports viewing and managing supported tokens.
7. **Token Swaps:** Provides swap functionality for supported assets and networks.
8. **Recovery Phrase:** Uses a Secret Recovery Phrase to restore wallet access.

### 3. Working of MetaMask
1. Install the MetaMask browser extension or mobile application.
2. Create a new wallet and set a secure password.
3. Securely back up the Secret Recovery Phrase offline.
4. MetaMask generates a wallet address and manages the associated cryptographic keys.
5. Connect the wallet to a supported DApp.
6. When a transaction is initiated, review its details, network and fees.
7. Approve the transaction. The wallet signs it and submits it to the network.

**Working flow:**
User → MetaMask Wallet → Connect to DApp → Review and Sign Transaction → Blockchain Network

### 4. Applications
- Sending and receiving supported cryptocurrencies.
- Accessing decentralized finance (DeFi) applications.
- Connecting to NFT marketplaces.
- Interacting with decentralized applications and blockchain games.
- Swapping supported tokens.

### 5. Benefits
- Users retain control of their wallet keys.
- Convenient browser and mobile access.
- Supports interaction with many Web3 applications.
- Provides transaction review and approval.
- Supports multiple compatible networks.

### 6. Drawbacks
- Phishing websites and malicious approvals can cause loss of assets.
- Losing the recovery phrase may result in permanent loss of wallet access.
- Network gas fees may be high or variable.
- Users must understand networks, addresses and transaction approvals.
- A transaction sent to an incorrect address may be difficult or impossible to reverse.

### Conclusion
MetaMask is a widely used non-custodial wallet for managing digital assets and interacting with Web3 applications. Users must protect their recovery phrase and carefully verify every transaction.

## Q3. Explain Cryptocurrency Wallets and Their Types. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
A cryptocurrency wallet is a digital tool used to manage cryptocurrency and interact with blockchain networks. It does not physically store coins; it manages the cryptographic keys that provide access to digital assets recorded on the blockchain.

- **Public key/address:** Used to receive cryptocurrency.
- **Private key:** Used to authorize transactions and prove control over assets.

### 2. Types of Cryptocurrency Wallets
Wallets are commonly classified as hot wallets and cold wallets.

### A. Hot Wallets
A hot wallet manages keys in an internet-connected environment. It is convenient for regular transactions and DApp access.

**Types:**
1. **Web Wallet:** Accessed through a browser or online service. Example: a Coinbase exchange wallet.
2. **Desktop Wallet:** Installed as software on a computer. Example: Electrum desktop wallet.
3. **Mobile Wallet:** Installed on a smartphone. Examples: Trust Wallet and MetaMask Mobile.

**Advantages:** Quick access, convenient transactions and easy DApp connectivity.

**Disadvantages:** Greater exposure to phishing, malware and online attacks.

### B. Cold Wallets
A cold wallet keeps private keys offline, reducing exposure to internet-based attacks. It is commonly used for long-term storage.

**Types:**
1. **Hardware Wallet:** A physical device that securely manages private keys and signs transactions. Examples: Ledger and Trezor.
2. **Paper Wallet:** A physical record of a cryptocurrency address and private key. It requires careful creation and secure storage.

**Advantages:** Reduced online exposure and suitability for long-term storage.

**Disadvantages:** Physical devices or backups can be lost or damaged; hardware wallets may involve cost; transactions require additional steps.

### 3. Difference Between Hot and Cold Wallets

| Parameter | Hot Wallet | Cold Wallet |
|---|---|---|
| Connectivity | Keys are managed in an internet-connected environment. | Private keys are kept offline. |
| Accessibility | Easily accessible. | Comparatively less convenient. |
| Online exposure | More exposed to online threats. | Less exposed to online threats. |
| Usage | Frequent transactions. | Long-term storage. |
| Examples | MetaMask, Trust Wallet. | Ledger, Trezor, paper wallet. |

### Conclusion
Cryptocurrency wallets manage the keys needed to access digital assets. Hot wallets offer convenience for frequent transactions, while cold wallets reduce online exposure and are suitable for long-term storage.

## Q5. Explain Bitcoin as a Cryptocurrency. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
Bitcoin (BTC) is the first widely adopted decentralized digital cryptocurrency. It was introduced under the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto in a white paper published in 2008, and the network launched in January 2009. It enables peer-to-peer digital value transfer without requiring a central payment authority. Bitcoin uses blockchain technology and Proof of Work (PoW) consensus.

### 2. Working of Bitcoin
1. **Transaction Creation:** The sender creates a transaction using a Bitcoin wallet and specifies the receiver's address and amount.
2. **Digital Authorization:** The wallet uses the sender's private key to authorize the transaction.
3. **Broadcasting:** The transaction is broadcast to the Bitcoin peer-to-peer network.
4. **Verification:** Nodes check transaction validity, including signatures and whether the referenced outputs are unspent.
5. **Mining:** Miners select valid transactions and compete to find a block hash satisfying the network's Proof of Work target.
6. **Block Validation:** The proposed block is broadcast. Nodes independently validate it and its transactions.
7. **Confirmation:** Once accepted, the block is added to the blockchain. Further blocks provide additional confirmations.

**Transaction flow:**
Sender creates transaction → Wallet authorizes → P2P network → Nodes verify → Miners perform PoW → Valid block added → Confirmations

### 3. Features of Bitcoin
1. **Decentralization:** No single central authority controls the Bitcoin network.
2. **Peer-to-Peer:** Users can transfer bitcoin directly through the network.
3. **Transparency:** Transactions are recorded on a public blockchain.
4. **Cryptographic Security:** Digital signatures and hashing help secure transactions and blocks.
5. **Limited Supply:** The protocol sets a maximum supply of 21 million BTC.
6. **Divisibility:** One bitcoin is divisible into 100 million satoshis.
7. **Global Accessibility:** The network can be accessed over the internet, subject to local laws and infrastructure.

### 4. Applications
- Digital payments where accepted.
- Peer-to-peer transfers.
- Cross-border value transfers.
- Trading and investment.
- Holding as a digital asset.

### 5. Advantages
- Does not require a central payment operator.
- Publicly verifiable transaction history.
- Global peer-to-peer network.
- Predictable issuance rules and capped supply.

### 6. Limitations
- High price volatility.
- Confirmation time and fees can vary.
- Proof of Work mining consumes substantial energy.
- Loss of private keys can mean loss of access.
- Transactions are generally difficult to reverse.

### Conclusion
Bitcoin is a decentralized cryptocurrency that uses blockchain, cryptography and Proof of Work to support peer-to-peer digital transactions without a central authority.

## Q6. Differentiate Between Custodial and Non-Custodial Wallets. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
Wallets can be classified according to who controls the private keys.

- **Custodial wallet:** A third-party service provider holds and manages the user's private keys.
- **Non-custodial wallet:** The user controls the private keys and is responsible for protecting them.

### 2. Difference Between Custodial and Non-Custodial Wallets

| Parameter | Custodial Wallet | Non-Custodial Wallet |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Private key control | Controlled by a third-party custodian. | Controlled by the user. |
| 2. Responsibility | Provider manages key custody. | User protects keys and recovery phrase. |
| 3. Access | Usually through an account with the provider. | Through the wallet and its recovery mechanism. |
| 4. Recovery | Provider may offer account recovery. | Recovery generally depends on the recovery phrase or backup. |
| 5. Transaction process | Provider's platform manages custody and transaction workflow. | User authorizes transactions with their wallet. |
| 6. Dependency | Depends on the service provider. | Does not require a custodian to control the keys. |
| 7. Risks | Provider failure, account restrictions or security breach. | Loss of keys, recovery phrase or signing-device security. |
| 8. Examples | Coinbase or Binance exchange account wallet. | MetaMask, Trust Wallet, Ledger. |

### 3. Custodial Wallet
- The provider controls the private keys on behalf of the user.
- Users normally access assets through a registered account.
- The provider may provide password reset and account recovery.
- Example: Cryptocurrency held in a centralized exchange account.

### 4. Non-Custodial Wallet
- The user controls the private keys.
- The user is responsible for safely backing up the recovery phrase.
- No custodian is required to authorize transactions on the user's behalf.
- Examples: MetaMask and hardware wallets such as Ledger.

### Conclusion
Custodial wallets offer convenience and provider-assisted account recovery, while non-custodial wallets give users direct control over their keys and greater responsibility for security. The main difference is who controls the private keys.

## Q7. Explain Hot Wallet and Cold Wallet Storage. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
Cryptocurrency wallets manage the keys required to access digital assets. Based on the environment in which private keys are managed, wallets are broadly classified as hot wallets and cold wallets.

### 2. Hot Wallet Storage
A hot wallet manages private keys in an internet-connected environment. It is convenient for frequent transactions, trading and DApp interaction.

**Types:**
1. **Web Wallet:** Accessed through a browser or online service. Example: a web-based wallet.
2. **Desktop Wallet:** Installed on a computer. Example: Electrum.
3. **Mobile Wallet:** Installed on a smartphone. Examples: MetaMask Mobile and Trust Wallet.

**Advantages:**
- Quick access to funds.
- Convenient for frequent transactions.
- Supports online DApp interaction.

**Disadvantages:**
- Greater exposure to phishing and malware.
- Compromised devices or accounts can put assets at risk.

### 3. Cold Wallet Storage
A cold wallet keeps private keys offline. It is commonly used to reduce online exposure and protect assets intended for longer-term storage.

**Types:**
1. **Hardware Wallet:** A dedicated physical device that stores keys securely and signs transactions. Examples: Ledger and Trezor.
2. **Paper Wallet:** A physical record of an address and private key. It must be generated and stored carefully.

**Advantages:**
- Reduced exposure to internet-based attacks.
- Suitable for long-term storage.
- Keys can remain offline during transaction preparation.

**Disadvantages:**
- Less convenient for frequent transactions.
- Hardware devices may have a purchase cost.
- Physical devices and backups can be lost, stolen or damaged.

### 4. Difference Between Hot and Cold Wallets

| Parameter | Hot Wallet | Cold Wallet |
|---|---|---|
| Internet | Keys managed in an online environment. | Keys kept offline. |
| Accessibility | Easily accessible. | Requires additional steps. |
| Security exposure | More exposed to online threats. | Less exposed to online threats. |
| Usage | Frequent transactions. | Long-term storage. |
| Cost | Often free. | Hardware devices may cost money. |
| Examples | MetaMask, Trust Wallet. | Ledger, Trezor. |

### Conclusion
Hot wallets prioritize convenience and quick access, whereas cold wallets prioritize keeping private keys offline and reducing online exposure.

## Q9. Explain Cryptocurrency Usage and Its Relevance with Examples. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
Cryptocurrency is a digital or virtual asset that uses cryptographic techniques to secure transactions. It commonly operates on a blockchain or distributed ledger and can be transferred electronically between users.

### 2. Usage and Relevance

1. **Digital Payments:** Cryptocurrency can be used to pay for goods and services where accepted. Example: Paying a merchant using Bitcoin.
2. **International Transfers:** Digital assets can be transferred across borders through supported networks. Example: Sending bitcoin to a recipient in another country.
3. **Investment and Trading:** Users buy and sell cryptocurrencies as digital assets. Example: Trading Bitcoin or Ether on an exchange.
4. **Decentralized Finance (DeFi):** Tokens and smart contracts support lending, borrowing and decentralized exchanges. Example: Using an Ethereum-based DeFi application.
5. **Smart Contracts and DApps:** Native cryptocurrencies may pay network fees for executing smart contracts. Example: Paying gas fees in ETH on Ethereum.
6. **Digital Ownership and NFTs:** Tokens can represent unique digital assets or collectibles. Example: Purchasing an NFT using a supported cryptocurrency.
7. **Financial Access:** A person with internet access and a compatible wallet may access certain blockchain services without a conventional bank account. Example: Receiving a supported cryptocurrency into a wallet.

### 3. Relevance
- Provides an alternative form of digital value transfer.
- Supports peer-to-peer transactions.
- Enables blockchain-based financial applications.
- Encourages innovation in digital assets and smart contracts.
- Enables tokenized representations of digital and real-world assets.

### 4. Limitations
- Cryptocurrency prices can be volatile.
- Users face phishing, hacking and wallet-security risks.
- Laws and regulations vary between jurisdictions.
- Network fees may change with demand.
- Incorrect or unauthorized transfers can be difficult to reverse.

### Conclusion
Cryptocurrency is used in digital payments, transfers, trading, DeFi, smart contracts and digital ownership. It creates new possibilities for digital finance, but users must consider volatility, security and regulatory risks.

## Q10. Describe Tokens in Cryptocurrency with Suitable Examples. [6 Marks]

### 1. Introduction
A cryptocurrency token is a digital asset created on an existing blockchain network. It can represent utility, value, governance rights, ownership or access to a service. Tokens are commonly created and managed using smart contracts and token standards.

**Example:** USDT, UNI and BAT are examples of tokens issued on blockchain networks.

### 2. Features of Tokens
1. **Digital Representation:** Represent value, rights, assets or services.
2. **Blockchain-Based:** Recorded and transferred on an existing blockchain.
3. **Transferability:** Can be transferred between compatible wallets.
4. **Smart Contract Support:** Many tokens are created and managed using smart contracts.
5. **Programmability:** Can support specific functions within decentralized applications.
6. **Transparency:** Transactions can be verified on the underlying blockchain.

### 3. Types of Cryptocurrency Tokens

1. **Utility Tokens:** Provide access to products, services or functions within an ecosystem. Example: BAT in the Brave ecosystem.
2. **Security Tokens:** Represent investment or financial rights in an underlying asset and may be subject to securities regulation. Example: A token representing a share in a tokenized real-estate investment.
3. **Governance Tokens:** Allow holders to vote on certain project or protocol decisions. Example: UNI of Uniswap.
4. **Stablecoins:** Designed to maintain a relatively stable value, often against a fiat currency. Examples: USDT and USDC.
5. **Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs):** Unique tokens representing a particular digital or physical asset or collectible. Example: A unique digital artwork NFT.

### 4. Difference Between Coin and Token

| Parameter | Coin | Token |
|---|---|---|
| Blockchain | Has its own native blockchain. | Created on an existing blockchain. |
| Purpose | Payments and native network operations. | Utility, governance, ownership and other functions. |
| Creation | Requires a blockchain protocol. | Commonly created through smart contracts or token standards. |
| Dependency | Native to its blockchain. | Depends on the underlying blockchain. |
| Examples | BTC, ETH. | USDT, UNI, BAT. |

### 5. Applications of Tokens
- Payments and transfer of digital value.
- Access to blockchain platform services.
- Voting in decentralized governance.
- DeFi applications such as lending and exchanges.
- Representing NFTs and tokenized assets.

### Conclusion
Cryptocurrency tokens extend blockchain functionality by representing utility, governance, stable value, ownership and other rights. They are an important part of decentralized applications and digital asset ecosystems.
