### Unit at a Glance
- Government: digital identity, land records, voting, PDS, subsidies, procurement and certificates.
- Banking and finance: cross-border payments, trade finance, smart contracts, KYC, fraud monitoring, securities settlement, loans and tokenization.
- Healthcare: EHR, medical data sharing, drug tracking, clinical trials, patient identity, insurance and remote monitoring.
- IoT: device data, gateways, blockchain validation, smart contracts and monitoring.
- Cross-domain applications: finance, healthcare, government, retail, IoT, energy, education and real estate.
- Retail: supply chain, authentication, payments, inventory, loyalty rewards, customer identity, returns and warranties.
- Voting: transparent and auditable vote records, but privacy, coercion, device security and accessibility remain challenges.
- Supply chain: product registration, suppliers, manufacturing, transport, distribution, retail and customer verification.
- Energy and utilities: P2P trading, renewable energy, smart grids, billing, EV charging and energy certificates.
- Cloud and other domains: off-chain cloud storage with blockchain hashes/references; AI, Big Data, IoT and cybersecurity.
- Digital identity: decentralized credentials, cryptographic verification, user control, access management and audit trails.

### Important Working Flows

**Government land records**
Citizen application → Document verification → Transaction submission → Network validation → Ledger update → Record verification.

**Banking international payment**
Customer → Sending bank → Blockchain validation → Distributed ledger → Receiving bank → Settlement.

**Healthcare record sharing**
Patient → Hospital EHR → Hash/reference on blockchain → Patient authorization → Doctor verification → Audit record.

**Blockchain + IoT**
IoT devices → Gateway → Blockchain validation and ledger → Smart contracts → Applications/users.

**Blockchain voting**
Registration → Authentication → Encrypted vote → Validation → Vote recording → Counting and verification.

**Supply chain**
Supplier → Manufacturer → Distributor/logistics → Retailer → Customer; blockchain records are shared with participants.

**P2P energy trading**
Solar producer → Energy trading platform → Smart contract → Buyer; physical electricity still flows through the grid.

**Cloud integration**
Users/apps → Cloud storage and processing → Blockchain hash/reference → Integrity verification.

**Digital identity**
User → Identity provider → Credential proof/reference on blockchain → Service provider verification → Authorized access.

### Key Benefits
- Transparency and traceability.
- Cryptographic security and tamper-evident records.
- Data integrity and auditability.
- Reduced dependence on a central authority.
- Reduced paperwork and reconciliation.
- Smart-contract automation.
- Improved coordination and trust among participants.

### Common Challenges
- Scalability and transaction throughput.
- Implementation and maintenance cost.
- Privacy and regulatory compliance.
- Integration with legacy systems.
- Technical complexity and skilled-resource requirements.
- Accuracy of data entered at the source.
- Key management and endpoint/device security.

### Important Technical Points
1. Blockchain protects recorded data against unauthorized alteration; it does not prove that the original input was true.
2. Large healthcare, IoT and cloud files are generally kept off-chain; hashes, references or access proofs may be recorded on-chain.
3. Blockchain does not itself transmit electricity; the physical grid delivers it.
4. Blockchain does not make online voting automatically secure or secret. Coercion, device compromise and ballot secrecy must be addressed.
5. Blockchain does not prevent phishing, malware or theft of private keys.
6. Smart contracts automate only the conditions and data supplied to them; incorrect inputs can still cause incorrect outcomes.
7. Blockchain integration complements cloud computing; it does not simply replace cloud storage.
