# Unit IV – Quick Revision

**Software Testing and Quality Assurance (410245-D) | SPPU | Fourth Year Computer Engineering**

## 1. Core Definitions

- **Software Defect:** A fault in a software work product that may cause incorrect behaviour or failure to meet requirements.
- **Quality Assurance (QA):** Process-oriented activity focused on defect prevention.
- **Quality Control (QC):** Product-oriented activity focused on evaluating products and detecting defects.
- **QMS:** Policies, processes, procedures and responsibilities used to manage quality.
- **Quality Plan:** Document defining quality objectives, standards, responsibilities, activities and monitoring methods.
- **Customer Satisfaction:** Degree to which a product meets or exceeds customer expectations.
- **CMM:** Framework for assessing and improving software process maturity.
- **ISO 9001:** Standard specifying requirements for a Quality Management System.

## 2. Error, Defect and Failure

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Error | Human mistake |
| Defect | Fault introduced into a work product |
| Failure | Incorrect behaviour observed during execution |

Memory sequence: Error → Defect → Failure.

## 3. ISO 9000 Family

| Standard | Remember |
|---|---|
| ISO 9000 | Fundamentals and vocabulary |
| ISO 9001 | QMS requirements; certifiable |
| ISO 9004 | Sustained success guidance |
| ISO 19011 | Management-system auditing guidance |

Seven principles: Customer Focus, Leadership, Engagement of People, Process Approach, Improvement, Evidence-Based Decision Making and Relationship Management.

ISO certification does not guarantee defect-free software.

## 4. CMM – Five Levels

1. **Initial:** Unpredictable process.
2. **Repeatable:** Basic project management.
3. **Defined:** Standardized and documented processes.
4. **Managed:** Quantitatively measured and controlled processes.
5. **Optimizing:** Continuous improvement.

Limitations: Time, cost, documentation overhead, implementation difficulty and no guarantee of defect-free products.

## 5. QA vs QC

| QA | QC |
|---|---|
| Process-oriented | Product-oriented |
| Defect prevention | Defect detection |
| Proactive | Detection-oriented |
| Establishes standards and procedures | Evaluates products |
| Audits and process reviews | Testing and product inspection |

Memory: QA = Process + Prevention; QC = Product + Detection.

## 6. QMS Structure

Quality Policy → Quality Objectives → Roles and Responsibilities → Processes and Procedures → Documentation → Monitoring and Measurement → Corrective Actions → Continual Improvement.

**Pillars:** Customer Focus, Leadership, Engagement of People, Process Approach, Improvement, Evidence-Based Decision Making and Relationship Management.

**Important aspects:** Quality Planning, QA, QC, Quality Improvement, Documentation, Audits and Customer Satisfaction.

## 7. Quality Plan

**Components:** Objectives, Standards, Roles, QA Activities, QC Activities, Testing Strategy, Metrics, Documentation, Risk Management, Review and Improvement.

**Preparation steps:** Study requirements → Define objectives → Select standards → Assign roles → Plan QA/QC → Define metrics → Document → Monitor and update.

## 8. Customer Satisfaction

Factors: Functionality, Reliability, Performance, Usability, Security, Compatibility, Maintainability and Customer Support.

Measurement methods: Surveys, feedback, interviews, usage analysis, complaint analysis and CSAT.

CSAT example formula: (Positive responses / Total responses) × 100.

## 9. Characteristics of Software

- Developed, not manufactured.
- Does not physically wear out.
- Intangible.
- Highly complex.
- Easy to reproduce.
- Continuously changing.
- Requires maintenance.
- Often custom-built.

## 10. Product Requirements

- **Stated:** Explicitly specified by customer.
- **Implied:** Naturally expected but not directly stated.
- **Present:** Required in current environment.
- **Future:** May be required as needs and technology change.

## 11. Software Development Process

Requirement Analysis → Planning and Feasibility → Design → Coding and Implementation → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance.

## 12. Problematic Areas in SDLC

Requirements, planning and estimation, design, coding, testing, communication, deployment, maintenance, resources and skills.

Reduction methods: requirement validation, realistic estimates, design reviews, coding standards, testing, documentation, communication and risk management.

## 13. Product Criticality

| Type | Failure impact | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Safety-Critical | Injury or loss of life | Aircraft control |
| Mission-Critical | Mission cannot be completed | Satellite control |
| Business-Critical | Financial loss or business interruption | Banking system |
| Non-Critical | Minor inconvenience | Wallpaper application |

## 14. Two Software Quality Control Methods

- **Software Testing:** Dynamic execution of software to identify defects.
- **Reviews and Inspections:** Static examination of requirements, design, code and documents.
